# G. PROJECT R01 — GLOBAL REMITTANCE PRICE CONTINUITY AI (FIRST FULL EXECUTION)

## G1. Research identity and motivation

**Repo:** `remittance-price-intelligence`  
**Working title:** *Are Low-Cost Remittance Services Reliably Affordable? Service-Linkage Uncertainty, Longitudinal Quote Continuity and Machine-Learning Prediction Across Global Remittance Corridors.*  
**Primary geography:** Global corridor panel. Sub-Saharan Africa, East Africa and Uganda receiving corridors are **subgroups**, only if adequate eligible independent records exist. Do NOT restrict full study to Uganda. UK→Uganda and Uganda→Kenya are different sending-receiving directions.

**Key scientific problem:** At survey time a money-transfer service quote may cost <=3% of transfer value. Can we predict whether that specific **reobserved surveyed quote** remains <=3% in the **next actual calendar quarter**? How does service-identity definition and missing future observations affect inference? Does a model improve triage for analysts deciding which quoted services to recheck, under finite review capacity?

**Unit:** corridor × provider × defined service bundle × transfer basket × origin quarter. Repeated units clustered. The threshold relates to SDG 10.c but the service-level classification is NOT the official aggregate SDG metric. Quote is a surveyed offer, **not** transaction price paid by an actual user, provider exit, household welfare or real-time execution opportunity.

## G2. Data and acquisition

- Official World Bank **Remittance Prices Worldwide** (RPW) data catalogue: `https://datacatalog.worldbank.org/search/dataset/0037898/remittance-prices-worldwide`; official methodology `https://remittanceprices.worldbank.org/methodology`.
- Prior retrieved workbook contained 204,469 post-2016 rows, over 300 corridors, collection to 2025 Q3; 2026 catalogue update is not 2026 observation. Confirm current actual content before use.

- Build full source manifest, observed vs imputed rates, schema changes by quarter, missing entire corridor-quarter, duplicates, negative reported prices (retain separately with documented sensitivity), zero costs, outliers, currency/basket conversions and apparently changed provider names.

## G3. Longitudinal identity and estimands

**Primary key candidate ('core'):** ordered corridor, provider, sending/payment method, access point, receiving/pickup modality/location, local transfer amount or stable basket identifier, and quarter. Resolve explicit provider normalization conservatively; no speculative fuzzy links counted as confirmed. Separate attributes (transfer speed, network reach) from identity in *core* key.

**Sensitivity 'strict bundle':** also requires unchanged speed and receiving-network coverage. Restrict comparisons to the **same original eligible records** before comparing definitions. Earlier pilot: 14,081 common initial low-cost records, 13,082 core reobserved, 10,150 strict reobserved, 2,934 core-only records almost all with changed speed, and 999 core-unobserved; do not hardcode counts as results of fresh run.

**Three estimands:**

1. `P(cost_t+1 <= 3% | cost_t <= 3%, same service reobserved in exactly quarter t+1)` — conditional price continuity (primary predictive target).
2. Bounds/sensitivity for all initially cheap eligible origin services **accounting for not reobserved**. Missing ≠ exit or more expensive.
3. Corridor-level probability of at least one **surveyed** <=3% service in adjacent surveyed quarters, conditional on surveillance coverage; NOT consumer access probability.

Define alternative horizon (2 quarters) and 5% threshold as explicitly secondary. Treat more than one transfer basket as different products unless principled equivalence exists.
