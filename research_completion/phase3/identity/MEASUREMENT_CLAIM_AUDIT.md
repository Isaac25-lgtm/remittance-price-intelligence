# Measurement claim audit

Among 36,429 uniquely valid core successor pairs, continuity is 0.81105712 (81.11%). Among all eligible origin records, its missing-outcome identification interval is [0.65161105, 0.84820149]. These are different targets. [M03]

Corridor survey monitoring gives 8,189/8,879 = 92.23% continuity of at least one cheap surveyed quote. Its 9,168 eligible origin corridor-quarters include 289 unknown follow-up corridor surveys. Phase 3 excludes 270 right-censored final-wave corridor-quarters from the earlier 9,438-origin display; the observed follow-up denominator and conditional rate are unchanged. It does not measure persistence of the same offer and is not SmaRT. [M06]

E4 over all primary eligible records is 81.3599% for unique core reobservation and 80.3410% for unique valid-cost reobservation. This denominator differs from the earlier conditional E4 modelling population. [M07]

The numerator in E1 excludes unknown successors. E2 retains ambiguous duplicate origin records, so its denominator is recorded offers, not a deduplicated count of latent services. The known-stay lower bound supports a majority of eligible records under either stated rule, but does not establish at least 80% continuity for all affordable origins. The conditional estimate is compatible with both all-origin bounds; its actual bias cannot be signed from these data.

SmaRT is a qualifying-service corridor cost indicator including speed/access criteria, not our same-record transition probability ([World Bank Issue54](https://datacatalogfiles.worldbank.org/ddh-published/0037898/DR0095413/RPW_main_report_and_annex_Q325.pdf), pp 9-10). Our E3 is an any-cheap-surveyed-quote transition and is also not the official SDG statistic. Do not subtract these rates as if they estimated the same population effect. The new interpretation is a caution about denominator and identity sensitivity, not proof official monitoring is erroneous.
